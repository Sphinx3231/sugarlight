# Blood sugar awareness: verified facts (gathered 2026-11-19)

Every number used on the blood sugar site comes from this list. Quotes were read from the source page on the date above.

## Global (IDF Diabetes Atlas, 11th edition, 2025; data year 2024)
Source: https://diabetesatlas.org/ and https://idf.org/about-diabetes/facts-figures/
- 589 million adults (20–79) live with diabetes, which is 1 in 9 (11.1%).
- Projected to reach 853 million by 2050 (1 in 8), an increase of 46%.
- Over 4 in 10 are unaware they have it: 252 million undiagnosed.
- 4 in 5 (81%) live in low- and middle-income countries.
- 3.4 million deaths in 2024, which is 1 every 9 seconds.
- At least USD 1 trillion in health expenditure (up 338% over 17 years).
- Over 90% of people with diabetes have type 2.

## WHO fact sheet (https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/diabetes)
- People living with diabetes rose from 200 million (1990) to 830 million (2022). Note: this counts adults 18+, a different method from IDF.
- 14% of adults 18+ had diabetes in 2022, up from 7% in 1990.
- 59% of adults 30+ with diabetes were not taking diabetes medication in 2022.
- Diabetes causes blindness, kidney failure, heart attacks, stroke and lower-limb amputation.
- Healthy diet, regular activity, normal body weight and avoiding tobacco prevent or delay type 2.
- Type 1 cannot currently be prevented.

## CDC: test ranges (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/diabetes-testing/index.html)

| A1C | below 5.7% | 5.7–6.4% | 6.5% or above |

## CDC: symptoms (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/signs-symptoms/index.html)
- Frequent urination; increased thirst and hunger; losing weight without trying; fatigue; feeling irritable or moody; blurry vision; frequent UTIs or yeast infections.
- Type 1 can appear suddenly, in weeks or months. It may add nausea, vomiting, stomach pains and DKA.
- Type 2 may add slow-healing cuts, dark skin patches (neck, armpits, groin), and numbness or tingling in the hands or feet.
- Some people notice no symptoms at all.

## CDC: low blood sugar (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/about/low-blood-sugar-hypoglycemia.html, .../treatment/treatment-low-blood-sugar-hypoglycemia.html)
- Below 70 mg/dL is low. Below 55 mg/dL is severely low.
- Symptoms: fast heartbeat, shaking, sweating, nervousness or anxiety, irritability or confusion, dizziness, hunger.
- The 15-15 rule: have 15 g of carbs, wait 15 minutes, re-check, and repeat if still under 70. Then eat a balanced snack.
- 15 g examples: ½ cup of juice or regular soda; 1 tablespoon of sugar, honey or syrup; 3–4 glucose tablets.
- Young children may need less. Ask a doctor.
- Severe lows need glucagon and emergency care.

## CDC: prevention (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/prevention-type-2/prediabetes-prevent-type-2.html)
- More than 2 in 5 American adults have prediabetes.
- Lifestyle changes can cut type 2 risk in half.
- The National DPP lowers risk by 58% (71% if over 60).
- Recommended: lose 5–7% of body weight and get 150 minutes a week of brisk activity (30 min × 5 days).

## CDC: US total (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/php/data-research/index.html)
- 40.1 million people (12.0%) had diagnosed or undiagnosed diabetes in 2023.

## CDC: risk factors (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/risk-factors/index.html)
Your risk of prediabetes and type 2 diabetes is higher if you:
- have overweight or obesity;
- are age 45 or older;
- have a parent or sibling with type 2 diabetes;
- are physically active less than 3 times a week;
- have non-alcoholic fatty liver disease;
- have had gestational diabetes, or a baby over 9 pounds;
- are African American, Hispanic or Latino, American Indian or Alaska Native. Some Pacific Islander and Asian American people also have higher risk.

## USPSTF: screening (https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes)
- August 24, 2021, grade B. Screen for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes in asymptomatic adults aged 35 to 70 who have overweight or obesity.
